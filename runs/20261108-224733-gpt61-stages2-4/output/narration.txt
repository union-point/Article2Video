Searchbloom publishes this agency list and ranks itself first, so its ranking reflects the publisher's own assessment, not an independent verdict. Throughout this guide, the source is Searchbloom: its facts come from company websites, and its case-study figures are the companies' own reporting.

Start by defining success: leads, sign-ups, or revenue from AI referrals, rather than citation counts or visibility scores. Then ask for a dated AI search result with a named or described company, a figure, and a period. Watch for promises of AI citations in weeks, or reports that count citations without mentioning leads or revenue.

AI answers are probabilistic: repeating the same question can produce different brands. Track a fixed set of real buyer questions daily across the AI platforms your buyers use, then read the trend. Separate branded and non-branded prompts, and segment them by awareness, consideration, and purchase. Ask which tracking tool, prompts, platforms, and run frequency an agency uses.

In Searchbloom's own tracking, about nine of every ten sources AI cites are on other people's sites. Ask which outside URLs are retrieved and cited for your top buyer questions, and which placements the agency can help you secure. When an answer leaves your business out, inspect the pages it cited; often, the fix is a listing through outreach, a review, or sponsorship.

Crawler access, correct structured data, and clean architecture make a site legible to AI platforms. The agency should handle technical, content, and entity work, and review visibility tracking with you monthly. Clarify whether the agency implements technical changes or supplies technical recommendations, and price the developer time you will need to provide.

Your team must own sponsorship and affiliate budgets when the most-retrieved roundup in your category is a paid placement. Your team must also own retailer, marketplace, and partner listings. That responsibility extends to social media and community work, brand positioning, and the budget behind PR.

In Searchbloom's comparison of nine companies, three publish a fee, two a minimum, and four a contract term. Before comparing agencies, get any unpublished fees, minimums, and contract lengths in writing.
