Investigated run: `20261108-224733-gpt61-stages2-4`. ## Finding

Two separate problems explain the result: the retained Whisper HTTP response already contains zero-duration word timestamps, and the local alignment policy escalates any such word to even timing for the entire scene. Timestamp parsing does not introduce the zero spans. The text partitioning itself matches the approved narration very closely.

**Count correction:** there are **31 zero-duration words out of 353 (8.8%)**, distributed across **16 of 20 sentences** in six scenes. The 16 `ZERO_DURATION_WORD` findings are sentence-level findings generated by `any(...)`, not a count of anomalous words.

## How our alignment amplifies the timestamp defects

1. Sentence partitioning consumes every transcript word once, in order. Nineteen sentence scores are 1.0; the remaining score is approximately 0.9833, and overall normalized text similarity is approximately 0.9989. There are no low-score, quantity, name, overlap, or whole-transcript mismatch findings in this run. Text similarity measures recognition agreement, not acoustic timing accuracy.
2. Each sentence initially receives the first word start and last word end (`article_video/alignment.py:129–131`). All 20 resulting sentence spans are positive, ordered, and inside their scenes, despite the individual zero-duration words.
3. Any zero-duration word creates a sentence finding (`:132–134`). Transcript validation intentionally permits equality (`article_video/transcription.py:33` rejects end < start, not end == start), allowing the diagnostic to reach alignment.
4. Any sentence finding triggers fallback for the entire scene (`article_video/alignment.py:168–176`). Every sentence is then given an equal share of scene time, regardless of its word count or available timestamp span. Claim-linked element times are also replaced by even timing; sparse word cues are suppressed (`:177–190`).
5. This changes 18 sentence intervals, including two with no zero-duration words: `scene_006.n1.s1` and `scene_007.n2.s1`. Sixteen affected sentences therefore lead to six scenes with degraded timing.
6. Scene cuts are computed before fallback, from the first word of each next scene (`:160–164`). Fallback does not improve or replace these cuts; the six fallback scenes still use the same ASR-derived boundaries. Every scene-opening word in this run has a positive duration.

Equal spacing can move sentence endpoints substantially. For example, `scene_004.n1.s1` ends at 69.820 seconds in the word timestamps but at 71.907 seconds after fallback, a 2.087-second shift. The following sentence starts at 70.720 seconds in the transcript but is delayed to 71.907 seconds by even spacing. These shifts are relative to the ASR timestamps; they are not measurements of error against the actual speech.

## Offline diagnostic experiment

In memory only, restored every sentence to its original first-word/last-word span, recomputed claim-linked elements and sparse cues using the existing functions, and retained the original scene cuts and raw transcript. All seven scenes then passed the existing structural timing validator, including their generated sparse cue bounds. No word timestamp was repaired or synthesized. This shows scene-wide equal spacing is not required to obtain structurally valid sentence/element spans for this input. It does **not** establish acoustic accuracy or clear the review findings.

Seven sentences have a zero-duration first or last word, so some outer sentence boundaries also remain uncertain. Simply suppressing the warning or declaring the diagnostic result valid would conceal that uncertainty.

## Recommended correction

- Separate transcript text-match quality, word timestamp quality, and sentence/scene boundary quality. Add explicit anomalous-word counts and indices; retain original aligned boundaries alongside any fallback boundaries.
- Preserve positive, monotonic sentence spans when defects concern internal word timing. Retain review status, and suppress or flag cues that depend on invalid word intervals. Diagnose zero-duration boundary words separately rather than automatically replacing the whole scene.
- Use even fallback only where sentence boundaries are unusable, or text matching fails. Require bounds/ordering checks on candidate sentence and scene spans, plus checks on each emitted cue. Keep existing protections for mismatches and cross-scene overlaps.
- If reliable word-level timing is required, evaluate a separate audio-to-approved-text alignment step or reviewed boundary corrections against the WAV. Do not manufacture short word durations or infer accuracy from a high text score.
- Recompute local alignment from the existing cached transcript after any implementation change, bumping the alignment version for provenance. A parsing change or another paid Whisper request is not needed to diagnose or address the over-broad fallback policy.
- Add regressions for multiple zero-duration words within one sentence, internal versus boundary zero spans, and preserving clean sentences/cues while retaining timestamp review findings. The existing zero-duration test explicitly expects whole-scene fallback, so current behavior is intentional policy rather than an accidental branch.

## Limits and preservation

The retained response proves the zero spans originate upstream of our parser, at the transcription service response boundary. It cannot reveal the service’s internal cause or prove whether surrounding nonzero word times match the audio. No listening/forced-alignment ground truth was established in this investigation.
Original timing, transcript, audio, approval, cache, and implementation files were preserved. No external API requests were made. This investigation adds only this report.
